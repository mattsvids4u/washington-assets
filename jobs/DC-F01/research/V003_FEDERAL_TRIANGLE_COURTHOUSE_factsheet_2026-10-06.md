V = verified (the publisher is quoted in the summary); P = probable; A = approximate; NF = not found.

The route (brief, WASHINGTON-2026-09-22-v001) runs along Pennsylvania Ave from 15th St to Peace Circle
(C2–C5), then onto the Capitol grounds. These are background buildings, so the faces that can be seen from Pennsylvania
Ave get the detail.

## 1 Post Office Department Building (1934), now the William Jefferson Clinton Federal Building (North)
- 12th St and Pennsylvania Ave NW, Federal Triangle. Delano & Aldrich, built 1931–35. HABS DC-814 (LoC dc0948). [V LoC/HABS]
- Modelled on the Place Vendôme. Its 12th St **hemicycle** forms half of the 12th Street Circle, linking the Grand Plaza
  (east–west) to 12th St. The IRS hemicycle that was to form the other half was **never built**. [V SAH DC-01-FT05]
- "Tri-unit" plan: two huge back-to-back semicircular units with side wings at the centre. [V GSA/SAH]
- Three-storey middle section, with a central projecting **Ionic pavilion** and two end pavilions of the same order. A **sidewalk-
  level arcade** carries the sidewalk around the circle, under arches at the projecting ends of the half-circles. [V SAH]
- Roof: a **slate mansard** at attic level, with dormers trimmed in green copper. [V SAH]. Note: this is not a red tile roof
  (the C20 kit does not apply here).
- On the Grand Plaza (west) side: a colonnade (HABS photo 17). [V HABS caption]
- Pennsylvania Ave (north) front and its exact bay count: NF. Photo needed.
- 1963: the Post Office Department headquarters (it was renamed only later). In 1963 the Grand Plaza was a parking lot. [P]

## 2 Internal Revenue Service Building (1930)
- Constitution Ave between 10th and 12th Sts NW. Office of the Supervising Architect under Louis A. Simon, built
  1928–35. Occupied 1 Jun 1930, the first Federal Triangle building to open. HABS DC-747 (LoC dc0747). [V]
- French Renaissance or classical. The Constitution Ave front gets the most elaborate treatment: the upper floors are
  dominated by a **three-storey Doric colonnade**, with a balustrade between the columns, carved limestone eagles, and an
  inscription over the main entrance. There are **end pavilions**. [V GSA/SAH; HABS photos 29, 31 "end pavilion"]
- An **unfinished facade at 11th St** remained into 1991 (HABS photo 16 caption). The planned 12th St hemicycle was never built.
  The 1963 north and west sides were therefore partly unfinished. [V HABS caption; the detail needs a photo]
- Roof: NF. Federal Triangle buildings usually have red tile hipped roofs, so it is [A] until a photo is checked.
- Seen from the route: only obliquely, across the Old Post Office block and the Grand Plaza, from Pennsylvania Ave between
  10th and 12th. Background LOD only, except the faces seen from the plaza. [A]

## 3 U.S. Courthouse (1952), now the E. Barrett Prettyman U.S. Courthouse
- 333 Constitution Ave NW, Reservation 10, bounded by Constitution Ave (S), 3rd St (E), C St (N) and John Marshall Pl (W). Louis
  Justement, built 1948–52; Truman laid the cornerstone 27 Jun 1950; it opened Nov 1952. [V GSA/FJC]
- **H-plan**: an **eight-storey** rectangular block crossed by two perpendicular **six-storey** wings on the east and west
  facades. Limestone and granite. [V GSA via Wikipedia/GSA]
- Stripped classicism with Moderne leanings: **no pediments, entablatures, porticoes or carved decoration**. [V GSA]
- The main front faces **south** across Constitution Ave to the Mall and the National Gallery. [V]
- **Do not model the William B. Bryant Annex (Michael Graves, 2005)** on the east side. Modern photos include it. [V]
- Seen from the route: the north and west faces from Pennsylvania Ave and John Marshall Pl (C5). [A]
- In 1963 its name was "United States Court House" (Prettyman name 1997). [V]
