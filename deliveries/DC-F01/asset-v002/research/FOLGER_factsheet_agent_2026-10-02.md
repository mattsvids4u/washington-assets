1 Site
- Full block, south side of East Capitol St between 2nd and 3rd Sts SE; main facade faces north onto East Capitol [V S1,S2 xs]
- Lot = northern half of Square 760 (Grant's Row, 14 row houses razed 1929) [V S7]
- South: rear courtyard faced an alley; across it the LoC Annex (standing in 1963) [V S5 / P S19]
- West faces 2nd St SE and the Capitol; Puck "looks west toward the US Capitol" [V S13]
- Reliefs "set off by a broad terrace and lawn" [V S9]; "rectangular-shaped terrace containing a shallow lawn ~25 ft wide by 135 ft long along the north elevation" [V text S4 xs; doc 2017-22]
- Cret forecourt: white marble staircases, benches, planters, wall along the front of the terrace, marble + bluestone pavers [V S14]
- "main entrance grandly raised on a marble plinth" [V S1]; sidewalk setback NF (>= 25 ft lawn) [A]

2 Dates / what did NOT exist in Nov 1963
- Paul Philippe Cret with consulting architect Alexander B. Trowbridge (from Sept 1929); completed 1932 [V S1,S9]; cornerstone 1930 [V S7]
- Period of significance 1929-32; DC Landmark 1964; NRHP 1969 (ref 69000294, listed 23 Jun 1969 [P S18])
- PRESENT in 1963: 1958-59 one-storey utilitarian office addition with storage below grade inside the open space of the U-shaped plan, plus a terrace [V S5,S8]
- NOT present: Hartman-Cox Bond Reading Room (late 1983) [V S5]; Elizabethan Garden (1989) [P]; aluminium Puck (2001-02; marble original in place in 1963) [V S13];
  2019-24 KieranTimberlake/OLIN renovation (Adams Pavilion under front lawn, sunken east/west entry plazas + ramps, tapestry gardens, poem wall, new Puck fountain, aluminium quotation letters) [V S2,S14,S15,S22]
- West vehicle drive/carriage entry probably existed in 1963 [P S24 xs]

3 Dimensions
- "226 feet from east to west by 111 feet from north to south and rises to a height of 48 feet" (68.88 x 33.83 x 14.63 m) [V S3 xs]
- "long, three-story building sits atop a raised plinth" [V S2]; plinth/terrace/attic heights, window sizes NF

4 North (East Capitol) facade
- "smooth, white marble walls divided into bays by long, narrow windows with Art Deco-inspired aluminum grilles separated by fluted pilasters"; nine window bays [V S2/S3 xs; S5 fluted pilasters]
- exhibition gallery behind, running nearly the width of the facade [V S6 xs]
- pilaster count/capital NF (8 or 10 [A]); bay spacing ~15 ft if nine bays match the 135 ft lawn [A]
- nine marble bas-reliefs by John Gregory, carved 1932 by the Piccirilli Brothers, each "six-by-six foot squares", below the windows [V S10 / P S20]
- order (viewer left to right): Midsummer Night's Dream, Romeo and Juliet, Merchant of Venice, Macbeth, Julius Caesar, King Lear, Richard III, Hamlet, Henry IV Part 1 [V ends / P middle S12]
- relief rights undetermined (1932; renewal/notice question) -> reference only, stand-ins
- top: "A broad attic story broken only by inscriptions, a slight recession at the top, and an entablature of shallowly incised abstract classical ornament at the bottom" [V S6 xs]; no projecting cornice reported [A]
- front inscriptions: Samuel Johnson "This therefore is the praise of Shakespeare That his drama is the mirrour of life"; Ben Jonson "Thou art a monument, without a tombe, And art alive still, while thy booke doth live, And we have wits to read, and praise to give" [V S23 xs]; positions NF; First Folio spellings required [V S9]
- building name chosen "Folger Shakespeare Library" [V S7]; position NF
- end entrances near each end from the terrace [P]; "large Art Deco window and door grilles are aluminum" [V S4 xs]; comedy/tragedy masks over entrances [P]
- "cast aluminum grillework, balustrades, and windows" [V S11]; light fixtures NF

5 West end (2nd St SE)
- "The west facade facing the Capitol is treated more prominently, as the main approach to the building is from the west" [V S6 xs]
- "two similar facades" (repeats the East Capitol composition) [P S24 xs]
- Puck: Brenda Putnam 1932, marble, on a fountain facing west; base "Lord, what fooles these mortals be!" [V S13,S22]
- theatre is NOT at the west end; west wing = Founder's Room, board suite, Registrar, Main Stair, service [V S3/S4 xs]

6 East end (3rd St SE)
- "The east facade is a blank wall broken only by masks of comedy and tragedy" [V S6 xs]; east wing holds the theatre [V S3/S6 xs]

7 South (rear)
- 1932: "U-shaped building with a courtyard between the arms on the south, rear side ... facing an alley", reading room in the centre of the U [V S5,S6]
- 1963: courtyard filled by the 1958-59 one-storey addition + terrace [V S5,S8]
- materials list "Georgia marble, glazed brick, aluminum, steel, glass, and concrete"; marble on primary elevations only -> rear/courtyard walls probably glazed brick [A from S4 xs]

8 Roof: NF; probably flat behind the attic with the "slight recession at the top" [A S6]; reading room has a high trussed roof inside (visibility NF)

9 Materials
- white Georgia marble ashlar "primarily in alternating rows of larger and smaller panels" [V S4 xs, S1]; plinth marble [V S1]; terrace marble + bluestone [V S14]
- backing brick salvaged from the razed row houses [V S7]; exterior metal all aluminium, not bronze [V S11,S13]
